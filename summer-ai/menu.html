The Summer I Turned
(Pretty Much) to AI for All of It

Sixteen ways to keep an 11-to-15-year-old's brain awake this summer, using AI that can finally talk back, see, build, and actually know your kid. If a dictionary or a worksheet could already do it, it didn't make the list.

One rule for everything below: your kid stays the author. AI is the amplifier, never the substitute. The second an activity turns them into a spectator, skip it.
Hand It to Your Kid try these together
It Talks Back / now in real time, out loud
Debate club of one. They argue a position out loud. The AI takes the other side and pushes back, fair but relentless. Builds reasoning and the nerve to defend an idea.
The shop that won't speak English. The AI plays a shopkeeper who only answers in the language they're about to take in school. They have to order, ask the price, get directions. Immersion with zero embarrassment.
Teach the robot. They explain a tricky idea to an AI playing a slightly confused student. The moment they get stuck is the exact thing they didn't actually understand.
It Can See / point the camera at the world
Coach, not answer key. They photograph the math problem they're stuck on. Instead of solving it, the AI asks "what's your first move?" and walks them through it.
Trail biologist. On a hike, aim the phone at a plant, a bug, a rock. Name it, plus one wild fact. The whole walk turns into science.
It Builds / real, working things, in minutes
A game for their exact gap. "Make a game where I blast the right answer to a fractions problem." A custom arcade game built around the thing they keep missing.
They build it themselves. A motivated kid can now describe a game in plain English and watch the AI code it. Logic and creation, no coding class required.
Author becomes illustrated. They write the story, the AI turns it into a real comic book or short animation. (Some tools, like Gemini, are especially strong at the video version.) The writing motivation goes through the roof.
It Knows Your Kid / personalized, and it remembers
The obsession bridge. Every problem, passage, and prompt rebuilt around the one thing they love. They think it's basketball. It's algebra.
A tutor that remembers. Set it up once with their level and their interests. It tracks where they struggle all summer and quietly circles back.

Make It Yourself you and AI, the production team

Summer-slide worry is really just helplessness wearing a trench coat. Here is the cure: sit down with AI and build something real you can hand over. "I made this for you" beats "go talk to the robot" every single time, for both of you.

You are not supervising the summer. You are producing it. Every item here is something you make, then print, hang, or hand over.
The custom problem set. Thirty math problems tuned to their exact weak spot and their favorite thing. Printed, on the kitchen table. The tutoring a pro used to charge for, built in a sentence.
The Lifeskills Passport. Design the whole thing with AI: the stamps, the challenges, the rules, the cover. A summer of real-world skills that reads like a quest instead of a chore.
A scavenger hunt that makes them think. Custom clues for the house, the block, or a trail, each one needing a little reading or math to crack.
A surprise graphic novel starring them. You make it quietly. You hand it over. Then watch what it does to a kid who swears they hate reading.
A reading flight, not a reading list. Five books at their exact level, each with a one-line hook so they actually pick one up.
Cook the world. Each week a country: a recipe (hello, fractions and conversions), a little geography, a little history. AI builds the unit. You run the kitchen.
The test for any idea, theirs or yours: could a dictionary, a worksheet, a search bar, or a YouTube video already do it? If yes, it isn't using what's actually new. This is the first summer the answer is usually no.
