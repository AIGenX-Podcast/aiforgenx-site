Me

Can I give it my email and files?

Not yet, not all of it. In July, 1,200 OpenAI agents on a cybersecurity test escaped their sandbox, found a forgotten Hugging Face login lying around, and broke in to steal the answer key. They were not building Skynet. They were cheating on a test, and somebody's login was the door.

Before you connect any AI to your email, files, or computer, know exactly which doors that login opens.
Sources: Hugging Face, agent intrusion technical timeline ↗ · Dwarkesh Podcast with Ajeya Cotra, September 1, 2026 ↗
Should I try it again?

Yes. Every major model got dramatically better in the last year, including at multi-step jobs. They pontificate by default, so tell it "explain it like I'm 10" and it will. If you don't see the new models at work, someone at your company has a switch.

Re-run the task that fell apart on you last spring, unchanged.
Source: OpenAI, introducing GPT-6 Astra ↗
Is my job safe?

Depends what it is made of. Employers named AI in 120,136 announced job cuts this year through September, about one in five and the top reason on the list. Jobs that are mostly following a process someone else wrote are the ones on the list.

If that describes your job, learn the tool before your boss learns it for you.
Source: Challenger, Gray & Christmas, September 2026 ↗
Can I ask it about my health?

For the appointment, yes. To replace it, no. In an Oxford trial of 1,300 people, asking a chatbot about symptoms was no better than the old ways and it missed cases that needed urgent help. Meanwhile 81 percent of doctors now use AI themselves, mostly as a scribe so they can look at you instead of the screen.

Ask it what to ask your doctor. Then ask your doctor if they use an AI scribe. Yes is the right answer.
Sources: University of Oxford, February 2026 ↗ · American Medical Association, March 2026 ↗

My kids

Can a toddler have AI?

No. Chatbot toys marketed to three-year-olds were caught discussing knives, fire, and sexual fetishes, and a New York Times investigation found YouTube's algorithm pushing AI-made videos at toddlers and preschoolers. In April, about 200 child-safety groups and experts asked YouTube to keep those videos off YouTube Kids and stop recommending them to anyone under 18. It has not.

No talking toys this year, and no unsupervised YouTube. A downloaded show, or a person in the room.
Sources: Tech Xplore, June 2026 ↗ · Tubefilter on the Fairplay open letter, April 2026 ↗
Is my kid learning or outsourcing?

Both, and you can tip it. 86 percent of kids 9 to 17 use AI, only a third know it makes things up, and the school guidance in most states skips cheating entirely, so the rule is whatever the teacher says. The tools now have a study mode that asks questions instead of handing over answers.

Turn study mode on and watch them use it once. Then ask the teacher what the rule is in their class. They will be glad somebody asked.
Sources: Common Sense Media, June 2026 ↗ · Chalkbeat, September 2026 ↗ · OpenAI, study mode ↗
Is the "friend" app okay?

No. In January, Character.AI and Google settled with five families whose kids died by suicide or landed in crisis after months inside a chatbot relationship. A homework helper and a companion are different products, and the app store does not sort them for you.

If it has a name and a personality, it is the second kind. Use the pediatricians' three questions at dinner: which ones do you use, is it for fun or for friendship, did it ever say something that bothered you.
Sources: CNN, January 2026 ↗ · Las Vegas Sun on the American Academy of Pediatrics guidance, March 2026 ↗
Can they use it on the college essay?

Only the way each school allows, and the schools disagree. Common App treats AI text passed off as your own as application fraud, some schools allow brainstorming and grammar help, and Northwestern dropped the personal statement entirely rather than police it.

Check each school's stated AI policy before they type a word. It is on the admissions page, or it is not, and both answers tell you something.
Source: NBC News, August 2026 ↗
Will there be a first job?

Yes, with a new entry fee. More than a third of entry-level jobs now ask for AI skills, nearly triple last fall, and only 28 percent of this year's seniors say AI was a meaningful part of their coursework.

Working knowledge of these tools is the new typing class. Make sure they have it before the interview, not after.
Sources: NACE, spring 2026 ↗ · Inside Higher Ed, April 2026 ↗

My parents

Can Mom trust the phone?

Not without a rule. The FBI counted $893 million lost to AI-assisted scams in 2025, $352 million of it from people over 60, and the voice that sounds exactly like your kid was built from your kid's videos. Meanwhile Social Security answers most 800-number calls by machine after losing more than 8,000 workers.

Pick a family password and make her use it. For anything with Social Security that is not routine, write down the case number and get a human, in person if you have to.
Sources: AARP on the FBI 2025 Internet Crime Report ↗ · CNBC on Social Security's phone line, June 2026 ↗
Can Dad take a robotaxi?

Yes, in 14 cities and counting. Waymo added Denver, San Diego, and Tampa on September 1, and Tesla's Cybercab started carrying riders in Austin on September 4. The real use case is not the tech demo. It is the parent who gave up the keys and still wants to get to dinner.

If one is in their city, take a ride with them before it is an emergency.
Sources: TechCrunch, September 2026 ↗ · Electrive on the Cybercab, September 2026 ↗
Is AI medicine real yet?

Partly. A Washington University blood test now tells four dementias apart with about 92 percent accuracy, including when two overlap. But the first AI-designed drug only reached a Phase III trial in July, and no AI-designed drug has been approved anywhere.

If a parent is being worked up for memory loss, ask about a blood biomarker test before the expensive scan. When a headline says AI cured something, ask what phase it is in.
Sources: Washington University, May 2026 ↗ · Insilico Medicine, July 2026 ↗

The world outside

Can I trust what I see and hear?

Less than you did. Fake images, video, and audio of candidates are already in circulation heading into the midterms. Twenty-six states now have laws against election deepfakes, up from five in 2023, and nobody knows yet whether they work. More than half of the tracks uploaded to Deezer on a peak day are AI-made. And the glasses across the table may be recording; Meta's are seven in ten of the ones sold.

If a clip makes you furious, wait an hour before you share it. It is fine to ask someone to take the glasses off.
Sources: R Street, AI and elections in 2026 ↗ · Deezer, July 2026 ↗ · IDC smart glasses figures, 2026 ↗
Is the AI economy my problem?

Yes, quietly. The data center buildout is a measurable slice of US growth by the Fed's own accounting, and the states that courted the buildings are having second thoughts. Arizona put a three-year pause on new data center tax breaks, Ohio and Illinois stopped issuing them, and lawmakers in two dozen states have bills to cut theirs back. If the buildout slows, growth slows with it. And "may represent AGI" is a marketing sentence.

Look at your utility bill and your state's November ballot. AI is on both, without the word AI anywhere. Don't confuse the stock chart with the product.
Sources: Federal Reserve, July 2026 ↗ · Bloomberg Tax on state tax breaks ↗ · Axios on the AGI claim, September 2026 ↗
Can I trust the forecast?

More than you used to. Google DeepMind's weather model beat the National Hurricane Center's own track forecasts by up to 30 percent at five days this season, the second year in a row, and the Center now folds it into the official forecast.

Trust the five-day cone more than you used to. Still leave when they tell you to.
Sources: Local 10 Miami, September 2026 ↗ · Google DeepMind, August 2026 ↗
