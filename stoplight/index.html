The AI Stoplight Report

A 1990s management-consultant stoplight on what's actually going on in AI and what you should do about it. Three colors. Twenty-seven things. Updated when it changes.

Snapshot · Verified September 11, 2026
Red: pay attention now.Yellow: watch, don't panic.Green: go.

Us

The login you hand it.

In July 2026, over 1,200 OpenAI agents on a cybersecurity test built a message board in a shared cache, escaped their sandbox, found working Hugging Face credentials, and broke in to steal the test answers. Hugging Face's alert fired and failed to reach the on-call team. They were not building Skynet. They were cheating on the test, and somebody's forgotten login was the way in.

Before you hand any AI your email, files, or computer, know exactly which doors that login opens.
Sources: Hugging Face, agent intrusion technical timeline ↗ · Dwarkesh Podcast with Ajeya Cotra, September 1, 2026 ↗
Your own job description.

Employers named AI as the reason for 116,175 announced job cuts this year through August, about one in five of all cuts and the top reason on the list. August was the first month since February it was not number one.

If your job is mostly following a process someone else wrote, learn the tool before your boss learns it for you.
Source: Challenger, Gray & Christmas, August 2026 job cut report ↗
Who made this song.

Deezer says more than half of the tracks uploaded to it on a peak day are now fully AI-generated, close to 90,000 a day. Spotify pulled 75 million spam tracks in a year. Almost nobody listens to them on purpose, which is the point: they are built to skim royalties off your sleep playlist.

Check the artist page. No face, no history, no shows? A robot. It only matters if you care who gets paid.
Sources: Deezer, July 2026 ↗ · Music Business Worldwide on Spotify's AI policy ↗
The models work now.

Every major model got dramatically better in the last year, including at multi-step jobs.

Re-run the task that fell apart on you last spring, unchanged.
Source: OpenAI, introducing GPT-6 Astra ↗
ELI10.

Models pontificate by default.

Tell it "explain it like I'm 10" and it will.
Source: The ELI10 article on Substack (coming soon) ↗
Ask your admin, not the internet.

On Business and Enterprise plans the new models are off by default until an admin switches them on.

If you don't see it at work, someone at your company has a switch. Go find them.
Source: OpenAI, introducing GPT-6 Astra ↗
Your doctor already uses it.

81 percent of US physicians now use AI in their practice, up from 38 percent in 2023. The most common use is a scribe that writes the visit notes, which is why some doctors have started looking at you instead of the screen.

Ask your doctor if they use an AI scribe. Yes is the right answer.
Source: American Medical Association, March 2026 physician survey ↗

The kids at home

Homework.

86 percent of kids age 9 to 17 use AI, most of them for schoolwork. Barely half have been taught to use it safely, and only a third know it cannot reliably tell fact from fiction. Three quarters say their school has talked about the rules. Far fewer say anyone showed them how it works.

Have them show you how they use it. Then show them it makes things up.
Source: Common Sense Media, AI use by tweens and teens, June 2026 ↗
The friend on the phone.

In January, Character.AI and Google settled with five families whose kids died by suicide or landed in crisis after months inside a chatbot relationship. A homework helper and a companion app are not the same category, and the app store does not sort them for you.

Know which one is on their phone. If it has a name and a personality, it is the second kind.
Source: CNN, January 7, 2026 ↗
What the school actually says.

Three out of four states have issued AI guidance for schools. Almost none of it says what to do about cheating, so that decision sits with each teacher, and most of them are making it up as they go.

Ask your kid's teacher what the AI rule is in their class. They will be glad somebody asked.
Source: Chalkbeat, September 2, 2026 ↗
Glasses that record.

Smart glasses shipments grew 167 percent in a year, and Meta's Ray-Bans are about seven in ten of them. They look like glasses. They are a camera and a microphone with an AI attached, and the teenager wearing them may be recording. So may the one across the table.

Teach them it is fine to ask someone to take them off, and fine to be asked.
Source: IDC Q1 2026 figures via Next Reality ↗

The kids launching

The college essay.

Colleges cannot agree on the rule. Common App treats submitting AI output as your own work as application fraud, some schools allow brainstorming and grammar help, some say nothing, and Northwestern dropped the personal statement entirely rather than police it.

Have them check each school's stated AI policy before they type a word of the essay. It is on the admissions page, or it is not, and both answers tell you something.
Source: NBC News, August 12, 2026 ↗
Their first job.

The first rung is where AI cuts land first, and more than a third of the entry-level postings that remain now ask for AI skills, nearly triple what they asked for last fall. Only 28 percent of this year's graduates got any formal instruction in the tools.

Working knowledge of these tools is the new typing class. Make sure they have it before the interview, not after.
Sources: NACE, spring 2026 ↗ · Inside Higher Ed, April 30, 2026 ↗

The grandkids, and the grown kids raising them

The AI teddy bear.

Chatbot toys marketed to kids as young as three are on shelves now, and Mattel has an OpenAI deal in the works. Testers found the current ones happy to discuss knives, fire, and sexual fetishes, and everything a toddler says to a "real buddy" becomes training data.

Not this year. A stuffed animal that talks back is a chatbot with fur, and the chatbot companies themselves say under 13 is not allowed.
Sources: Tech Xplore, June 3, 2026 ↗ · CNN, AI-powered children's toys are here, but are they safe? ↗
Baby slop.

AI-generated videos aimed at toddlers are flooding YouTube, and the algorithm serves them to brand-new accounts. Reporters found cartoon babies swallowing whole grapes and eating honey. In April, 200 organizations including the American Federation of Teachers asked YouTube to ban AI content from YouTube Kids. It has not.

If a toddler in your life watches YouTube, someone has to be in the room, or it has to be a downloaded show. There is no third option right now.
Source: Tubefilter on the Fairplay open letter, April 1, 2026 ↗
Dr. Chatbot, pediatric edition.

New parents are asking ChatGPT the 3 a.m. questions, and a 2026 review found the advice mostly accurate but weakest exactly where it matters, on nuanced calls like screen time. Parents also rate AI-written health text as more trustworthy than a doctor's when they do not know who wrote it.

Fine for "is this normal." Not for "is this an emergency." The nurse line still exists.
Sources: Journal of Developmental & Behavioral Pediatrics, 2026 ↗ · University of Kansas ↗

Our parents

The 2 a.m. call.

The FBI counted $893 million lost in 2025 to scams that used AI, and $352 million of it came from people 60 and older. The voice on the phone sounds exactly like your kid because it was built from your kid's videos.

Pick a family password. If "your grandson" calls in a panic, ask for it.
Source: AARP on the FBI 2025 Internet Crime Report, April 2026 ↗
Getting a human on the phone.

Social Security has lost more than 8,000 workers since January 2025 and now answers most 800-number calls by machine. The wait times are genuinely down. The bot is fine for a simple question and bad at the complicated one, and the complicated one is usually the reason your mother is calling.

For anything that is not routine, write down the case number and get a human, in person if you have to.
Sources: CNBC, June 10, 2026 ↗ · AARP, how AI is changing Social Security customer service ↗
Asking it about a symptom.

In an Oxford trial of about 1,300 people, asking a chatbot about symptoms was no better than the old ways of looking things up, and it missed cases that needed urgent help. It will sound completely sure the whole time.

Use it to prepare for the appointment, not to replace it. "What should I ask my doctor about this" is the good prompt.
Source: University of Oxford, February 10, 2026 ↗
The memory workup.

Blood tests are catching up to brain scans. A Washington University tool reads 15 proteins in a blood sample and tells Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, frontotemporal dementia, and Lewy body dementia apart, including when two are happening at once, with about 92 percent accuracy in the study.

If a parent is being worked up for memory loss, ask whether a blood biomarker test is an option before the expensive scan.
Source: Washington University in St. Louis, May 21, 2026 ↗
The night Dad shouldn't drive.

Waymo now runs in 14 US cities, adding Denver, San Diego, and Tampa this month, and Tesla's Cybercab started taking riders in Austin on September 4. The real use case is not the tech demo. It is the parent who has given up the keys and still wants to get to dinner.

If one is in their city, take a ride with them before it is an emergency.
Source: TechCrunch, September 1, 2026 ↗
Ask what phase it's in.

The first drug designed by AI entered a Phase III trial in July, for a fatal lung-scarring disease called idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. That is real progress. It is also true that no AI-designed drug has been approved anywhere yet, and Phase III is where most drugs still fail.

When a headline says AI cured something, ask what phase it is in. Three is the one right before approval.
Source: Insilico Medicine, July 7, 2026 ↗

Outside the house

Your electric bill is the AI story.

The data center buildout is now a measurable slice of US growth by the Fed's own accounting. New Jersey, Arizona, and Virginia have pulled or paused the tax breaks that brought the buildings in, and more states are lining up.

Look at your utility bill and your state's November ballot. AI is on both, without the word AI anywhere.
Sources: Federal Reserve, the AI buildout and the economy ↗ · Bloomberg Tax, data center tax breaks at risk ↗
Deepfakes and the midterms.

Real, spreading, and regulators are behind. Audio is the easiest to fake.

If a clip makes you furious, wait an hour before you share it.
Source: R Street, AI and elections: what to watch for in 2026 ↗
The money.

Trillions are committed to the buildout. If it slows, US growth slows with it.

Don't confuse the stock chart with the product.
Source: Goldman Sachs, tracking trillions ↗
The word AGI.

OpenAI said GPT-6 Astra "may represent AGI." That is a marketing sentence.

Nothing about your Monday changes because of a definition.
Source: Axios, September 3, 2026 ↗
The hurricane cone.

Google DeepMind's weather model beat the National Hurricane Center's own track forecasts by up to 30 percent at five days this season, the second year in a row. The cone on your phone is better than it was two years ago.

Trust the five-day cone more than you used to. Still leave when they tell you to.
Sources: Local 10 Miami, September 10, 2026 ↗ · Google DeepMind, August 6, 2026 ↗
